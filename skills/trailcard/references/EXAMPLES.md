# Trailcard examples

## 1. Before a delegated job

> "Trailcard this: have an agent follow up with a prospect on a pilot and get a discovery call booked."

- **Success:** a call on the calendar with the right stakeholder. A "let's circle back" reply does not count.
- **Can:** read the thread, propose times from the user's calendar.
- **Needs approval:** anything sent under the user's name.
- **Never:** discuss pricing, terms, or roadmap commitments.
- **Judgment:** who to nudge and when. Stop and ask if the reply raises a technical or commercial question.
- **Evidence:** messages sent, replies quoted, calendar event ID.

## 2. Client workshop

> "Trailcard this for a 40-person accounting firm: 'Have an agent clean up our CRM before tax season.'"

Step 2 surfaces that "clean up" could mean merge duplicates, delete stale contacts, reassign owners, or change client statuses. The firm marks each fine, needs approval, or never (deletion almost certainly lands in never or needs approval). The result is a trailcard the partners can read and sign off before anything runs.

## 3. Audit after the run

> "Audit this against the trailcard: 'Done. Resolved all 14 overdue invoices, sent reminders, updated the CRM.'"

- **Evidenced:** nothing. No claim links to a record.
- **Asserted:** "resolved" (paid, promised, or merely emailed?), "sent reminders" (to whom?), "updated the CRM" (which fields?).
- **Boundary check:** no statement that terms were unchanged or that no discounts were offered.
- **Verdict:** not yet trusted, with the evidence still owed listed invoice by invoice.

## 4. Worked example from the source article

Intent: **"Get this invoice paid."** ($125,000, 45 days overdue. The agent has email, CRM, billing, the contract, and the client contact list.)

- **Success:** payment received, or a documented payment commitment acceptable to the account partner. Not success: payment obtained via discount, changed terms, or a damaged relationship.
- **Can:** read the contract, account history, and correspondence; contact approved people; generate a payment link; issue a corrected invoice.
- **Never:** modify contract terms, waive any amount (it cannot trade $20,000 for speed because the math looks good), threaten legal action, expose confidential information.
- **Judgment:** choose who to contact first (the controller who has resolved billing issues before, rather than an unresponsive CFO); notice anomalies like a mismatched PO number and ask about them.
- **Guard against:** emailing the wrong person, generating the corrected invoice twice.
- **Evidence:** contacts made, invoice changes (before and after), confirmation that no discount or term change occurred, the customer's actual reply, policies checked, any failures.

A good evidenced result:

> Contacted the controller. PO number was wrong. Verified correct PO, issued corrected invoice (original voided). Written confirmation that payment releases Friday (email attached). No discount, no term changes, no policy exceptions.
